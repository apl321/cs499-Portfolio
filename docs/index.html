Professional Self-Assessment

Andrew Townsend · October 18, 2026

My educational and professional background has given me a somewhat different perspective than if I had entered the major directly. Before pursuing my B.S. in Computer Science at SNHU, I earned my B.S. in Chemical Engineering at UNH and gained professional experience in process engineering, manufacturing, and quality assurance. These experiences have influenced how I approach technical problems, particularly with regard to reliability, efficiency, and the potential consequences of design choices. Throughout the curriculum, I have developed a broader understanding of software development while also narrowing my professional interests toward cybersecurity.

When I began the program, I was uncertain about which area of computer science I wanted to pursue. Data analytics was an attractive destination due to my previous experience as a process engineer and experience in using data and analytics to solve problems. As I progressed, I became increasingly interested in cybersecurity. I especially enjoy the adversarial nature of cybersecurity, as it would force me to continually adapt to and anticipate new threats. My current goal is to transition into a cybersecurity role, potentially progressing into cloud security engineering. I am also interested in operational technology security, where my engineering background and experience with industrial processes could provide additional value.

My professional experience was greatly enhanced through collaboration and communication. Many of my most successful initiatives began as a conversation with an operator. Although engineers usually have a thorough understanding of a process, the operators who work with it every day have practical knowledge that cannot be fully captured through spreadsheets and documentation. Failing to take their experience into consideration often results in missed opportunities for improvement and the sort of tone-deaf decisions my profession is often criticized for.

Throughout the program I have also developed my understanding of algorithms and data structures. Much like engineering, efficiency, scalability, and ease of maintenance are important components of problem solving (though admittedly I have not had to do much scaling-up in engineering). My coursework in Python, SQL, and data analysis has broadened my understanding of how information can be organized and manipulated to solve problems, and should be of great use in analyzing the various trade-offs presented by multiple potential solutions. There is, of course, always room for improvement.

I believe software engineering in general as well as database development have been especially valuable areas of study. I recall being particularly frustrated with abstraction early on; processes often succeed or fail based on small details, so placing complicated procedures inside a black box and taking their functionality on “faith” was initially a difficult thing for me to accept. Over time, I was able to appreciate its necessity in managing complexity. Separating responsibilities and reducing complexity make larger applications easier to understand and maintain by reducing the need for the developer to understand every minute detail.

Security has become an increasingly important factor in how I evaluate software. Early in my coursework, I often treated security as an afterthought, an approach that has historically contributed to vulnerabilities in software development. The increasing sophistication of cyberattacks has reinforced the necessity of treating cybersecurity as a foundational part of software design rather than something to be added afterward. My coursework in secure coding has also taught me to consider how a system might be exploited as opposed to simply functioning as I intended it to. Authentication, input validation, and protecting sensitive information all need to be considered during the development process. A program with serious security flaws may well be best off not functioning at all.

All three enhancements build upon Travlr, a full-stack travel application originally developed in CS 465. The software engineering enhancement improves the application's structure, authentication, error handling, and maintainability. The algorithms enhancement adds functionality that allows users to search, filter, and sort trip information. The database enhancement expands upon its query capabilities, improves data validation, and connects the Angular interface to server-side searching, sorting, filtering, and pagination. Automated testing was also added to verify functionality, including authentication and database query parameters.

Being able to use the same project for all three enhancements allowed me to examine how these areas interact with one another. Changes to one part of the application often required corresponding changes in other areas, which I think reinforces the importance of taking a holistic approach to applications.

Completing this program has given me a foundation to continue developing my technical skills. When I graduated from my engineering major, our professors told us that now we were ready to learn engineering. I believe computer science is similar in that regard, and that the degree shows that you have enough of a foundation to begin learning how to make meaningful contributions to your team. Moving forward, I intend to build upon my education through certifications and independent projects, with the eventual goal of working in cloud security engineering. There is still much to learn, but I believe my professional experiences and education will put me in a position to succeed.
